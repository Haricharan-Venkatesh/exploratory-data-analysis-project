Empirical Validation Benchmarks & Known Scientific Limitations

Empirical Generalization Metrics

  • A. Unseen-Game Benchmark: ROC-AUC ≈ 0.9008 (PR-AUC: 0.4780, Balanced Acc: 81.3%) across 1,228 held-out games (82,214 moves).
  • B. Unseen-Player Generalization: ROC-AUC ≈ 0.8639 across 2,100 unseen accounts, proving the model detects move physics rather than memorizing players.
  • C. Paired Human-vs-Bot Holdout: ROC-AUC ≈ 0.5176 (Balanced Acc: 55.7%), demonstrating near-chance discrimination when engines intentionally emulate human moves.

Strict Temporal Architecture

Strict Zero Lookahead: Move k strictly evaluates historical telemetry (moves 1 to k). Total game length, future plies, engine eval evaluations, and game outcomes are rigorously quarantined.

No Identity Proxies: Zero reliance on Elo rating differences or player identifiers; classifications rely purely on clock timing and move deliberation physics.

Known Boundary Conditions & Failure Modes

  • D. Bullet Time-Scramble False Positives: In fast Bullet scrambles (<15s remaining), humans queue rapid pre-moves with near-zero latency, mimicking engine speed. Operational recommendation: use strict threshold τ ≥ 0.85 for time scrambles.
  • E. Anthropomorphic / Calibrated Bots: Neural engines (e.g. Maia Chess) trained on human distributions and injected with randomized delays (2–5s) deliberately mimic human pacing, representing a fundamental limit for pure move-time models.
